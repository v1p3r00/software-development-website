## Digital transformation is not one giant project

“Digital transformation” can sound like a much bigger undertaking than it actually needs to be.

It can seem as if a business needs a new website, CRM, ERP, mobile app, AI solution and cloud infrastructure all at once.

For most SMEs, that is not the sensible starting point.

**Digital transformation is usually a sequence of smaller improvements that build on each other.**

This is particularly relevant in Hungary. The European Commission's 2026 Digital Decade country report says that Hungarian SMEs continue to lag EU peers in the adoption of digital technologies across several areas. The Commission specifically recommends targeted measures to improve basic SME digitalisation. [European Commission – Hungary 2026 Digital Decade Country Report](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/hu/factpages/hungarys-2026-digital-decade-country-report)

The EU's 2030 target is for more than 90% of SMEs to reach at least a basic level of digital intensity. In 2025, the figure across EU SMEs was 71%. [Eurostat – Digitalisation in Europe 2026](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/en/web/interactive-publications/digitalisation-2026)

So the question is not necessarily:

> “Is my business digital enough?”

A more useful question is:

> “What is the next digital improvement that can produce a measurable business result?”

---

## Step 1: Get your website right

For many businesses, this is the logical starting point.

Your website may be the first digital interaction a potential customer has with your company.

It does not necessarily need to be a spectacular, expensive project.

Start with the basics:

- Is it obvious what you offer?
- Can visitors find your services?
- Does it work properly on mobile?
- Is it easy to contact you?
- Can people request a quotation or book an appointment?
- Can you measure where enquiries come from?
- Is the information accurate and up to date?

A modern website can go further.

It could include:

- online quotation requests,
- appointment booking,
- e-commerce,
- customer login,
- document uploads,
- calculators,
- product configurators.

**A website becomes a genuine business system when it stops simply presenting information and starts handling business processes.**

---

## Step 2: Find the repetitive manual work

Once the basic digital presence is working, look for opportunities to automate.

Do not start with:

> “What could we use AI for?”

Start with:

> “What do we do manually every week that software could handle instead?”

Look for things such as:

- copying emails into spreadsheets,
- preparing quotations manually,
- entering invoice data more than once,
- creating recurring reports,
- sending customer reminders,
- renaming and organising files,
- manually updating statuses.

A simple automation can often produce more value than an impressive system that is rarely used.

### A simple example

Manual process:

```text
Website
↓
Email
↓
Employee reads it
↓
Spreadsheet
↓
Quotation
↓
Email to customer
↓
CRM update
```

Automated process:

```text
Website
↓
CRM
↓
Automatic task
↓
Quotation template
↓
Sales notification
```

You do not necessarily need a completely new business system to achieve this.

## Step 3: Connect the systems you already have

Many SMEs do not have too little software.

They have **too many disconnected systems**.

For example:

- webshop,
- invoicing software,
- CRM,
- accounting system,
- stock management,
- spreadsheets,
- email platform.

Each application may work perfectly well on its own.

The problem is that employees have to move information from one system to another.

That is where integration becomes useful.

The EU's Digital Intensity Index measures the use of 12 different digital technologies, reflecting the fact that digital maturity is not about one technology in isolation. [Eurostat – Information on data: Digital Intensity Index](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/digital-economy-and-society/information-data)

A simple integration might look like this:

```text
Webshop
   ↓
New order
   ↓
Invoicing system
```

A more complex setup could look like this:

```text
Webshop
   ↓
Integration layer
   ├── CRM
   ├── Invoicing
   ├── Stock management
   └── Email platform
```

**The goal of integration is not to connect as many systems as possible. It is to remove unnecessary manual data movement.**

---

## Step 4: Look at AI after the basics are in place

AI can be extremely useful.

But not every business needs an AI project first.

If employees are still copying data from one spreadsheet to another, the better first step may be fixing the underlying process.

Once the data and workflows are in place, AI becomes much more interesting.

Potential applications include:

- initial customer-service processing,
- document summarisation,
- quotation preparation,
- internal knowledge search,
- email classification,
- product descriptions,
- report analysis,
- internal AI assistants.

AI adoption is growing across Europe, but it is still far from universal. One of the EU's 2030 targets is for 75% of businesses to use cloud computing, big data analysis or AI. [Eurostat – Digitalisation in Europe 2026](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/en/web/interactive-publications/digitalisation-2026)

Hungary has historically lagged the EU average in business AI adoption. For example, the European Commission's 2024 country report recorded AI adoption by Hungarian enterprises at 3.7%, compared with 8% across the EU for the relevant year. Later reports use different reference years, so these figures should not be treated as a direct year-on-year comparison. [European Commission – Hungary 2024 Digital Decade Country Report](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/hungary-2024-digital-decade-country-report)

The important point is not that every company needs AI immediately.

**It is that businesses should identify AI use cases where the outcome can actually be measured.**

## Step 5: Build custom software when it is justified

Eventually, ready-made applications and automation tools may no longer be enough.

For example:

- your workflow is highly specialised,
- several systems need to become one process,
- too much manual work remains,
- existing software requires too many compromises,
- you need a customer portal,
- you need a custom calculator or configurator,
- the software itself creates business value or competitive differentiation.

That is when custom development may make sense.

But **you do not necessarily need to build everything from scratch**.

A sensible architecture might look like:

```text
Off-the-shelf CRM
       +
Off-the-shelf invoicing
       +
Cloud services
       +
AI service
       +
Custom business application
       =
One connected system
```

In many cases, this is more sensible than rebuilding every individual function yourself.

## Do not start with technology. Start with the problem.

One of the most common digitalisation mistakes is starting with technology.

“We need an app.”

“We need an AI chatbot.”

“We need an ERP.”

“We need our own CRM.”

None of those statements is a business objective by itself.

Try defining the target instead:

- 50% less manual data entry,
- faster quotations,
- less administration,
- shorter customer response times,
- fewer data-entry errors,
- more online enquiries,
- faster reporting.

**When the problem is clearly defined, choosing the technology becomes much easier.**

## What should you digitalise first?

A simple way to prioritise processes is to consider four factors.

### 1. Frequency

Does it happen every day?

Every week?

Once a month?

The more often a process is repeated, the greater the potential value of automation.

### 2. Time

How long does it take?

A five-minute task has a very different business case from a three-hour process.

### 3. Error risk

Does someone frequently make mistakes?

Does the result need to be checked afterwards?

Manual data entry is often a good automation candidate.

### 4. Business impact

Does automation merely make the work more convenient?

Or does it affect revenue, customer service or operating costs?

A simple prioritisation model could look like:

```text
Impact × Frequency × Time saved
-------------------------------
      Implementation cost
```

This is not a precise financial model.

It is simply a useful way to avoid choosing projects based only on how impressive they sound.

## Start with quick wins

Not every digital project needs to take months.

Look for processes that are:

- clearly defined,
- repeated frequently,
- relatively self-contained,
- easy to test,
- easy to measure.

For example:

**Before**

```text
Quotation processing: 15 minutes / customer
20 customers / week
= 5 hours / week
```

**After**

```text
Automated pre-processing: 3 minutes / customer
20 customers / week
= 1 hour / week
```

Saving four hours a week is already a meaningful amount of time over a year.

And that is just one process.

---

## Measure whether the change actually worked

Digital transformation is not successful simply because a system has been launched.

It is successful when something has improved.

Before starting a project, define one or two measurements such as:

- processing time,
- response time,
- number of errors,
- manual working hours,
- online enquiries,
- conversion rate,
- customer-service workload,
- system usage.

For example:

```text
Starting point:
Quotation preparation → 45 minutes

Target:
Quotation preparation → 15 minutes

Result:
→ 67% time saving
```

Now the question is no longer:

“Is the new system good?”

It becomes:

**“Did it achieve the business outcome we wanted?”**

## Hungarian SMEs still have room to improve

Digitalisation is not a finished project.

In 2025, 71% of EU SMEs reached at least a basic level of digital intensity, while the EU's 2030 target is above 90%. [Eurostat – Digitalisation in Europe 2026](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/en/web/interactive-publications/digitalisation-2026)

The European Commission's 2026 Digital Decade report says Hungarian SMEs remain behind EU peers in technology uptake across several areas and recommends targeted measures to improve basic digitalisation. [European Commission – Hungary 2026 Digital Decade Country Report](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/hu/factpages/hungarys-2026-digital-decade-country-report)

That does not mean every business needs the same strategy.

A five-person professional services company has very different needs from an 80-person manufacturer.

**Digital maturity is not about how many applications you have. It is about how effectively technology supports the way your business actually works.**

## A realistic SME roadmap

If you are starting now, this can be a useful sequence:

```text
1. Website and online presence
          ↓
2. Digital core processes
          ↓
3. Automation
          ↓
4. System integration
          ↓
5. Data and reporting
          ↓
6. AI
          ↓
7. Custom software
```

You do not have to complete all seven steps.

A business may find that automation solves most of its problems and never need custom software.

Another business may discover through its integrations and automation work that a custom platform would eventually be more efficient.

---

## Do not try to digitalise everything at once

The biggest project is not necessarily the best first project.

A safer approach is:

1. choose one problem,
2. measure the current situation,
3. solve it,
4. measure the result,
5. learn from it,
6. move to the next problem.

That turns digital transformation from a one-off investment into a continuous improvement process.

### So where should you start?

Not necessarily with AI.

Not necessarily with an ERP.

And not necessarily with custom software.

**Start where your business currently loses the most time, money or opportunity unnecessarily.**

It might be your website.

It might be a spreadsheet.

It might be two systems that do not communicate.

It might be a three-hour manual process every day.

And it might genuinely be AI.

The goal is not to use as much technology as possible.

**The goal is to use technology to achieve better business results with less manual work and fewer errors.**
