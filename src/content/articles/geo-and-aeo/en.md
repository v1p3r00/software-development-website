## Search is changing from links to answers

People increasingly do not search by typing a keyword and opening ten blue links.

They ask a question.

> “Which web developer can build a custom customer portal for an SME?”

Or:

> “What is the best way to connect online quotations with appointment booking?”

AI-powered search systems and assistants can respond by gathering information from multiple sources and producing a synthesised answer.

That creates a new question for website owners:

**How can your website become one of those sources?**

This is where terms such as **GEO**, or Generative Engine Optimisation, and **AEO**, or Answer Engine Optimisation, come in.

But there is an important caveat.

There are plenty of marketing claims around GEO and AEO, while much of the actual ranking and retrieval process remains proprietary.

**There is no switch you can turn on that guarantees an AI system will cite your website.**

---

## What are GEO and AEO?

The terms are not used consistently across the industry.

AEO generally refers to creating content that directly answers questions in a clear and useful way.

Instead of:

> “Modern solutions for today's businesses.”

you might write:

> “A customer portal lets your customers view documents, orders, invoices and service status in one place.”

GEO is broader.

Generative Engine Optimisation is a research area concerned with increasing the likelihood that content appears in, is cited by, or contributes to answers generated by generative search systems. One of the early papers in the field introduced GEO as a framework for improving visibility in generative-engine responses. [arXiv – GEO: Generative Engine Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)

**AEO tends to focus more on answerability, while GEO focuses more broadly on visibility and influence within generative answers.**

In practice, the two overlap considerably.

---

## The biggest myth: there is no separate “AI SEO”

One of the most common claims is that websites now need an entirely new technical SEO system for AI search.

That is not what Google's current documentation says.

Google states that the same fundamental SEO practices continue to apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode.

There are no additional technical requirements.

There is no special AI markup.

There is no dedicated “AI SEO” file required to appear in these features. [Google Search Central – AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)

Google's guidance for generative AI features continues to emphasise familiar fundamentals:

- pages that can be crawled,
- important content available as text,
- useful and reliable content,
- sound technical SEO,
- structured data where relevant.

[Google Search Central – Optimizing your website for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)

That distinction matters.

**You do not need to throw away your SEO strategy because AI Search exists.**

You need to extend it by asking whether your content is also easy for AI systems to understand and use.

---

## So what actually helps?

Mostly the same things that make a website useful to people.

It should be:

- clear,
- accurate,
- relevant,
- current,
- well structured,
- verifiable,
- crawlable.

Google's people-first content guidance explicitly recommends creating useful, reliable content for people rather than content primarily designed to manipulate search rankings. [Google Search Central – Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

That principle remains relevant to AI Search.

If an article answers a question clearly, distinguishes facts from opinions, provides specific information and cites its sources, it is easier for both people and AI systems to interpret.

---

## 1. Answer real questions

AI answers start with questions.

So create content around genuine questions your customers ask.

For a web development company, that might include:

- How much does a custom website cost?
- When should a business choose a web application instead of a webshop?
- How can two business systems be connected?
- When is a customer portal worth building?
- How much does custom software cost?

These are more useful topics than:

> “Digital solutions for modern businesses.”

### A useful answer structure

```text
Question
↓
Short direct answer
↓
Explanation
↓
Example
↓
Evidence / source
↓
Related questions
```text

Not because this is an “AI hack”.

**Because it is good content structure for humans too.**

---

## 2. Make information easy to extract

A long paragraph can hide several different facts.

For example:

> “Our system can be used in many different business environments and can be configured in several ways depending on the individual requirements of each customer, which means that pricing is determined separately…”

Compare that with:

```text
Basic plan: €125 / month
Users: maximum 10
Implementation: 3–5 working days
Support: email
```text

The second version is easier for people to scan.

It is also easier for a machine to interpret.

A 2026 GEO study found that, within its analysed generative-search datasets, higher-influence source pages tended to be more structured, semantically aligned and richer in extractable evidence such as definitions, numerical facts, comparisons and procedures. This is research evidence, not a universal Google or ChatGPT ranking formula. [arXiv – From Citation Selection to Citation Absorption](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25707)

---

## 3. Use specific facts

AI-generated answers often benefit from information that can be expressed precisely.

For example:

- prices,
- dates,
- percentages,
- dimensions,
- durations,
- specifications,
- comparisons,
- definitions,
- procedures.

Not:

> “Very fast.”

But:

> “The average processing time is two working days.”

Not:

> “Many businesses use it.”

But:

> “71% of EU SMEs reached at least a basic level of digital intensity in 2025.”

Of course, only publish figures that you can support with a reliable source.

**GEO does not mean filling your articles with numbers. It means making claims specific and verifiable.**

---

## 4. Sources may become even more important

It is not only what you say that matters.

The evidence behind the statement can matter too.

For professional content, consider:

- primary sources,
- official documentation,
- research papers,
- publication dates,
- links to evidence,
- clear distinction between fact and opinion.

AI systems may combine multiple sources.

But source selection varies between systems.

A 2025 study comparing generative search systems found substantial differences in source selection, source diversity and sensitivity to query phrasing. [arXiv – Generative Engine Optimization: How to Dominate AI Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08919)

That is why there is no reliable universal rule such as:

> “Use these ten GEO techniques and AI systems will always cite you.”

---

## 5. Technical SEO still matters

AI Search does not make technical SEO obsolete.

Google still needs to be able to crawl and index your pages.

Check things such as:

- robots.txt,
- `noindex`,
- XML sitemaps,
- canonical URLs,
- mobile usability,
- page performance,
- HTTPS,
- internal links,
- HTTP status codes.

Google's technical requirements continue to state that Googlebot needs access to pages and that pages need to work correctly for them to be eligible for Search. [Google Search Central – Technical requirements](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials)

Page experience also remains relevant. Google highlights areas including Core Web Vitals, secure delivery, mobile presentation and avoiding intrusive elements as part of the overall page experience. [Google Search Central – Understanding page experience](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/page-experience)

---

## 6. Structured data helps, but it is not a magic button

Structured data helps search systems understand the content of a page.

It can be useful for:

- articles,
- products,
- organisations,
- events,
- local businesses,
- certain question-and-answer pages.

Google describes structured data as a standardised format for providing explicit information about the meaning of page content. [Google Search Central – Introduction to structured data markup](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data)

But:

**Structured data is not a GEO switch.**

It does not guarantee AI Overviews visibility.

It does not guarantee a first-page ranking.

It does not guarantee citation.

Google's structured-data guidelines explicitly state that structured data does not guarantee that a particular search feature will appear. [Google Search Central – General structured data guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies)

---

## 7. Make authorship and dates clear

For professional content, it is useful to make the following visible:

- author,
- author's expertise,
- publication date,
- update date,
- sources,
- references.

This is especially important for topics that change quickly.

For example:

> “Updated September 2026.”

is much more useful than an article with no date at all.

Google's current guidance also emphasises accurate, reliable, people-first content. [Google Search Central – Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

---

## 8. Your own website is not the only source

AI systems do not necessarily build a picture of your company from your website alone.

They may encounter:

- specialist publications,
- documentation,
- news,
- research,
- partner websites,
- interviews,
- directories,
- databases.

That means AI visibility is not necessarily an on-site SEO problem only.

A 2025 study found that earned media and third-party sources represented a significant part of the sources used by the generative search systems it examined, while also finding substantial differences between systems. [arXiv – Generative Engine Optimization: How to Dominate AI Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08919)

That does not mean you can “hack” AI systems with PR articles.

It means:

**Your professional reputation should not exist only on your own website.**

---

## 9. For ChatGPT, one technical setting is particularly relevant

AI Search is not limited to Google.

ChatGPT Search uses its own web crawling infrastructure.

OpenAI's documentation states that **OAI-SearchBot** is used to surface websites in ChatGPT search results.

If a website blocks OAI-SearchBot, the site will not appear in ChatGPT search answers, although it may still appear as a navigational link. [OpenAI – Overview of OpenAI Crawlers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)

OpenAI separates the roles of OAI-SearchBot and GPTBot.

That matters because:

**being available for ChatGPT Search is not the same thing as allowing your content to be used for model training.**

OpenAI also documents that publishers can track referrals from ChatGPT using analytics platforms, with a `utm_source=chatgpt.com` parameter added to referral URLs. [OpenAI – Publishers and Developers FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856)

---

## 10. What does AEO really mean?

Suppose someone asks:

> “How much does a custom CRM cost?”

A good answer page does not necessarily need to be a 3,000-word marketing article.

Its opening could be:

> **A custom CRM typically costs tens of thousands of euros, but the final price depends on user roles, integrations, business logic and required features.**

Then you can explain:

- what drives the price,
- examples,
- price ranges,
- common questions,
- evidence.

That is useful for AEO.

But in reality, it is simply **good professional content writing**.

---

## GEO is not keyword stuffing

Traditional SEO has long had a problem with keyword stuffing.

GEO can create the same temptation.

Someone may try to insert:

- AI,
- ChatGPT,
- generative AI,
- AI Search,
- GEO,
- AEO

into every paragraph.

That does not make the content more useful.

Generative search systems are not simply performing keyword matching.

A simplified conceptual model might look like:

```text
User query
↓
Search / retrieval
↓
Source selection
↓
Reranking
↓
Content processing
↓
Answer generation
↓
Citations
```text

The exact pipeline varies by system and much of it is not public.

That is why **you should not infer your chances of appearing in AI answers from one SEO metric.**

## So what should you actually do?

Here is a practical GEO/AEO checklist.

### Content

- Answer genuine customer questions.
- Start with direct answers.
- Use specific facts.
- Explain specialist terminology.
- Include examples.
- Update outdated information.

### Trust

- Cite primary sources.
- Identify the author.
- Show publication and update dates.
- Separate facts from opinions.
- Never publish unsupported statistics.

### Technical

- Keep important pages crawlable.
- Do not accidentally block content.
- Use relevant structured data.
- Maintain your sitemap.
- Build a sensible internal-link structure.
- Keep the site fast and mobile-friendly.

### AI Search

- Check crawler access for the AI services you want to appear in.
- Measure referral traffic.
- Test real queries involving your brand and topics.
- Inspect actual AI answers.
- Do not assume that a tactic working in Google will work identically across every AI system.

---

## What should you not believe?

**“Add llms.txt and ChatGPT will start citing you.”**

There is no general evidence for that rule.

**“Create special GEO pages and AI systems will rank you higher.”**

There is no universal technical requirement like that.

**“Schema markup guarantees AI Overviews.”**

No.

**“GEO replaces SEO.”**

No.

**“There is one GEO score that tells you how good your website is.”**

Current research treats the problem as involving multiple stages and outcomes: discoverability, citation, actual content use and downstream business impact. The terminology and methodology are still developing. [arXiv – Optimizing Visibility in Generative Engines: A Critical Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14035)

---

## The goal is not to trick the AI

This is probably the most important point.

GEO is not most useful when you discover a trick that persuades an AI system to mention you.

It is useful when your website:

- answers genuine questions,
- provides reliable information,
- is clear,
- is well structured,
- is crawlable,
- contains concrete evidence,
- cites trustworthy sources.

**The best GEO strategy looks remarkably similar to a good professional content strategy.**

The new part is the measurement question:

> “Was my page not only discovered, but actually used when the answer was generated?”

That is different from a traditional Google ranking.

---

## What might a good AI Search strategy look like in 2026?

Think of it as a chain:

```text
Good technical SEO
        ↓
Useful professional content
        ↓
Answers to specific questions
        ↓
Reliable sources
        ↓
Structured information
        ↓
Discoverability by AI systems
        ↓
Citation / content use
        ↓
AI-driven traffic
        ↓
Business outcome
```text

Several of the first steps have been good web practice for years.

The new element is that **visibility in AI systems and the business impact of that visibility can now be measured alongside traditional search performance.**

### So how do you get your website into AI answers?

There is no guaranteed recipe.

And it would be misleading to promise one.

Google's own documentation says that the fundamental SEO practices continue to apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode, with no special technical “AI markup” required for eligibility. [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)

A much better starting point is therefore surprisingly simple:

**Create content that gives a precise, useful and verifiable answer to a real question, then make sure search engines and relevant AI services can technically access it.**

That is less exciting than a “secret GEO hack”.

But it is much closer to what current official documentation and research actually support.
