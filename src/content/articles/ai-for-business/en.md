Artificial intelligence is no longer a technology only for large corporations. More and more tools are available that a smaller business can build into its day-to-day operations.

But the question is not **"should we use AI?"**

It is this:

> **Where can it create real business value – and where must it not be used?**

A well-chosen AI solution can cut down on admin, speed up customer handling, support sales, or free up time from tasks that used to be done by hand.

---

## AI is more than a chatbot

Most people think of artificial intelligence as a chat window.

In a business setting, though, it can be used far more widely:

- processing documents,
- categorising emails and enquiries,
- customer service,
- preparing quotes,
- supporting sales processes,
- producing reports and summaries,
- searching an internal knowledge base,
- creating content,
- analysing data,
- automating repetitive processes.

And the really interesting solutions do not use AI on its own; they connect it to the business's existing systems.

```text
CRM + AI + online shop + invoicing + email + internal database
```

That way AI is no longer a separate tool, but **part of a business process**.

---

## What does this mean in practice?

### 💇 Hair salon

AI can help answer frequent customer questions, manage bookings and prepare marketing content.

*Example:* someone asks for an appointment on Messenger, and the assistant offers the free slots based on the booking system.

### 🧱 Builder / construction business

Quote requests, emails and on-site notes can be turned into structured data.

*Example:* from a long email, AI picks out the work area, the square metres, the deadline and the tasks involved.

### 🍽️ Restaurant

AI can be used to handle bookings, customer questions, reviews and reports.

*Example:* the system summarises guest reviews and shows which problems come up most often.

### 🔧 Car mechanic

A structured job sheet can be created from the fault a customer describes.

*Example:* from the incoming message, AI picks out the car model, the symptom and the circumstances in which it occurs.

### 🏠 Estate agency

AI can help with property listings, customer communication and categorising enquiries.

*Example:* the system groups enquiries by whether the person wants to buy now, is planning for later, or is just asking for information.

### 📚 Accounting firm

Document processing is one of the most obvious uses of AI.

*Example:* the data needed can be extracted automatically from invoices and PDFs, and the accountant then checks it.

### 🛒 Online shop

AI can help with product content, customer service and personalised recommendations.

*Example:* a shopper describes in plain language what they are looking for, and AI recommends relevant options from the current product range.

---

## AI in sales

In sales, the problem is often not a lack of information but too much of it.

With hundreds or thousands of leads, it is hard to decide by hand which enquiry to follow up first.

AI can analyse, for example:

- previous contacts,
- emails,
- purchase history,
- CRM data,
- signals of interest.

Based on this, the salesperson can get a more relevant priority list.

---

## AI in document processing

Almost every business has PDFs, contracts, invoices, quotes and other documents.

AI can extract information from them and organise it. For example:

```text
PDF → AI → data → CRM / ERP / database
```

This is especially interesting when you have to work with large volumes of partly unstructured documents.

---

## AI in customer service

One of the most time-consuming parts of customer service is often answering the same questions again and again.

AI can handle the simpler enquiries while passing more complex cases on to a colleague.

The goal is not necessarily to replace people.

**It is to make sure the questions that reach people are the ones that really need a human decision.**

---

## AI in reporting and decision support

Businesses produce huge amounts of data, but it is not always easy to draw business conclusions from it quickly.

With AI, a manager can, for example, ask in plain language:

> "What changed in sales compared with last month?"

Based on the available data, the system can summarise the changes and highlight the most important differences.

---

## AI + automation

AI can be useful on its own, but in many cases it creates the most value when it is part of an automated process. For example:

```text
A quote request arrives
        ↓
AI processes it
        ↓
extracts the data
        ↓
saves it to the CRM
        ↓
creates a task
        ↓
notifies the salesperson
```

This is no longer a simple chatbot.

**It is an automated business process.**

## Where must AI not be used?

The use of AI is not unlimited.

For a business, it is especially important to distinguish whether something is:

- **prohibited**,
- **regulated**,
- or simply **too risky** without human oversight.

### 1. Prohibited uses of AI

The European Union's [AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689) ([Article 5](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/)) prohibits or strictly limits certain AI practices.

These can include, for example, certain:

- manipulative or deceptive techniques,
- systems that exploit vulnerable people,
- social scoring systems,
- biometric applications.

These prohibitions have applied since 2 February 2025, and the European Commission has also [published guidelines](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-prohibited-artificial-intelligence-ai-practices-defined-ai-act) on how to interpret them.

The legal assessment always depends on how the system actually works and how it is used.

So a business should not rely simply on the fact that an AI tool is "available".

### 2. Be careful with personal and confidential data

A business may handle a great deal of sensitive information:

- customer data,
- contracts,
- financial information,
- employee data,
- health data,
- trade secrets.

These should not simply be pasted into any public AI service. Where personal data is involved, the [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679) applies to the use of AI tools as well.

For example, an accountant should not upload client data to an unknown AI tool just because it is free.

Nor should a law firm send confidential contracts without proper data protection and security guarantees.

When choosing an AI system, the important questions are therefore:

- Where is the data stored?
- Is it used to train models?
- Who can access it?
- What data protection guarantees are there?
- What contractual terms apply to the data?

### 3. Do not blindly leave critical decisions to AI

An AI's answer can be convincing and still be wrong.

So it can be especially risky if AI decides without human oversight on, for example:

- credit,
- employment,
- health matters,
- legal matters,
- insurance,
- significant financial decisions.

Several of these areas – such as recruitment, creditworthiness assessment and life and health insurance – count as [high-risk under the AI Act](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/). Under the [amendment adopted in 2026](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/06/29/artificial-intelligence-council-gives-final-green-light-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/), the obligations for these systems apply from 2 December 2027. And [Article 22 of the GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-22-gdpr/) already restricts decisions based solely on automated processing that have legal or similarly significant effects.

For high-risk processes, the role of AI, human review and the legal requirements have to be defined when the system is being designed.

### 4. Do not use AI where a simple rule is better

Not every automation needs artificial intelligence. For example:

```text
New order → confirmation email
```

That does not need AI. However:

```text
Incoming customer message → interpretation → categorisation → finding relevant information → drafting a reply
```

is a task where AI can bring a real advantage.

Good digitalisation therefore does not start from:

> "Where can we use AI?"

But from:

> **"What is the simplest and safest technology for this problem?"**

### 5. Do not let AI communicate unchecked

A wrong AI answer in an internal document can be awkward.

A wrong answer to a customer is a business problem.

So it is worth defining in which cases:

```text
AI can decide → AI can suggest → a person checks → a person decides
```

For a simple customer service question, AI may even reply automatically.

For a question about an individual contract, a legal or a financial matter, though, human approval may be the sensible choice.

Under the AI Act's [transparency rules](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/), since 2 August 2026 customers generally also have to be told when they are talking to an AI system rather than a person.

## AI: a tool, not a goal

Introducing AI is not a digitalisation strategy in itself.

A business may need **AI**.

But it may instead need:

- a simple automation,
- a better CRM,
- a new website,
- system integration
- or custom software.

The right solution should always be derived from the problem.

---

## How should you start?

It is worth first looking at:

- Which process is repeated most often?
- Where does most admin time go?
- Where is a lot of unstructured data created?
- Where are we losing enquiries?
- Which process slows work down the most?

From these, a few areas usually emerge quickly where a smaller AI or automation project can already deliver measurable results.

---

## A good AI solution is not necessarily spectacular

There may be no futuristic chatbot at the end.

It may just be a system running in the background:

```text
email → AI → CRM → task → notification
```

The user sees almost none of it.

But the business does less manual work, responds faster and can process more information.

**That is the real business value of AI.**

---

## In summary

Artificial intelligence can be used for customer service, sales, document processing, preparing quotes, reporting, internal knowledge bases, marketing, data processing and automating business processes.

But it is just as important to know where it must not or should not be used.

A sound AI strategy therefore starts with three questions:

1. What do we want to make more efficient?
2. What data can we use safely?
3. Where is human control needed?

Once these are answered, AI is no longer a technology buzzword.

**It becomes a business tool.**
