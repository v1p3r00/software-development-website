If you want more potential customers to find your business through Google, you will eventually come across two obvious options: SEO and Google Ads.

They are often presented as alternatives.

In reality, they are two different tools designed to achieve different things.

SEO, or Search Engine Optimisation, is about improving your website's organic visibility in search. Google describes SEO as helping search engines understand your content and helping users find your site through search. ([Google Search Central – SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide))

Google Ads, on the other hand, is paid advertising. Search campaigns can put your ads in front of people who are actively searching for products or services like yours. ([Google Ads – Create a Search campaign](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9510373))

**So the question is not necessarily "SEO or Google Ads?" For many businesses, it is "which one makes sense at this stage, and could they work together?"**

---

## SEO: building organic visibility over time

SEO covers the work involved in making a website easier for search engines to crawl, understand and present to relevant users.

It can include:

- technical SEO,
- useful content,
- understanding search intent,
- internal linking,
- structured data,
- website performance,
- improving the overall usefulness and clarity of a website.

You do not pay Google simply for appearing in its organic results. Google explicitly states that it does not accept payment to rank sites higher in organic search. ([Google Search Central – Do You Need an SEO?](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo))

That does not mean SEO is free.

You may still pay for:

- SEO expertise,
- content writing,
- technical development,
- research,
- audits,
- digital PR or link-related work.

The cost is simply not a direct payment to Google for each organic click.

---

## Google Ads: paying for targeted visibility

Google Ads works differently.

You create a campaign, select the searches or audiences you want to target, set a budget and choose a bidding strategy. Google then uses an auction system to determine when and where your ads appear.

Search campaigns are specifically designed to reach people actively searching for products and services related to your keywords. ([Google Ads – Search campaigns](https://business.google.com/en-all/ad-solutions/search/))

Depending on the campaign and bidding model, you may pay per click.

With CPC, or cost-per-click bidding, you pay for clicks on your ads. ([Google Ads Help – Cost-per-click](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/116495))

The basic difference looks like this:

```text
SEO → content + technical work → organic visibility

Google Ads → budget + advertising → paid visibility → clicks
```text

---

## Which one produces results faster?

This is one of the clearest differences between the two.

A Google Ads Search campaign can be launched relatively quickly.

Once the campaign is active, relevant searches can trigger your ads without waiting for a new page to build up a history of organic visibility.

That can be particularly useful when launching:

- a new service,
- a seasonal offer,
- a new product,
- a time-sensitive campaign.

SEO usually works on a different timescale.

Google says that some changes to a website can be reflected in search within days, while others may take several months. It also makes clear that there is no guarantee that a particular SEO change will produce a noticeable improvement in rankings or traffic. ([Google Search Central – Core Updates](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates))

**That is why anyone promising a guaranteed first-place Google ranking should be treated with caution.**

---

## One has a direct click cost; the other has a work cost

It is easy to compare the two incorrectly.

With Google Ads, the cost is relatively visible.

For example:

```text
Monthly Ads budget → clicks → enquiries → customers
```text

With CPC bidding, you pay for clicks, and the actual CPC can be lower than your maximum CPC bid. ([Google Ads Help – Cost-per-click](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/116495))

With SEO, you do not pay Google for an organic click.

But you may still spend money on the work required to build and maintain organic visibility.

That could include:

- technical development,
- SEO consulting,
- content production,
- keyword and customer research,
- website improvements,
- digital PR.

So SEO is often more **time- and work-intensive**, while Ads has a much more direct media cost.

---

## When does SEO make sense?

SEO can be particularly useful when people regularly search for the products or services you offer.

For example:

- accountant in London,
- industrial maintenance company,
- web development services,
- air conditioning installation,
- wedding photographer.

The opportunity is to create useful pages that genuinely answer those searches.

Not because you are guaranteed to rank first.

But because **you can build an organic search presence that is not based entirely on paying for each individual click.**

Google's Search Central guidance describes SEO as helping search engines crawl, index and understand your content. ([Google Search Central – SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide))

---

## When does Google Ads make sense?

Paid search can be particularly useful when you want to generate traffic for a specific offer quickly.

### Launching a new service

You may not have much organic visibility for the topic yet, but you still want enquiries.

### Seasonal demand

You might be selling a product or service around Christmas, summer, tax deadlines or another specific period.

### Strong buying intent

People are already searching for the exact service or product you provide.

### Testing an offer

You can use paid traffic to test whether an offer generates interest before investing heavily in a longer-term content strategy.

Google's Search campaigns are specifically designed to reach people who are actively searching for relevant products and services. ([Google Ads – Create a Search campaign](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9510373))

---

## Google Ads does not automatically create customers

This is just as important as the previous point.

Paying for clicks does not mean that those clicks will turn into sales.

The journey looks more like:

```text
Ad → click → landing page → interest → enquiry → sale
```text

If the landing page is poor, a good advert cannot fix the entire process.

Imagine your advert says:

"Custom business website in two weeks."

But the click takes the visitor to a generic homepage with no information about the offer.

The connection between the search, the advert and the landing page is lost.

**Paid traffic is only useful when the website can turn that traffic into a meaningful next step.**

---

## SEO and Google Ads work on different timescales

A simple comparison:

| Factor | SEO | Google Ads |
|---|---|---|
| Traffic | Organic | Paid |
| Starting speed | Usually slower | Faster to launch |
| Direct click cost | None | CPC/PPC campaigns have a click cost |
| Long-term content value | Can build over time | Not the primary purpose |
| What happens when spending stops | Not directly applicable | Paid traffic can fall when the campaign stops |
| Long-term visibility | Strong focus | Less central |
| Campaign testing | Usually slower | Can be started relatively quickly |

This does not mean that SEO is "free" or that Ads is "bad".

They simply operate differently.

---

## What happens when you stop Google Ads?

This is an important business distinction.

If you run a CPC-based Google Ads campaign, you pay for clicks generated by the campaign.

If you stop the campaign, the paid traffic generated by that campaign will stop.

SEO is different.

If a service page or article has established organic visibility, people can continue to find it without an individual advertising charge for each click.

That does not mean organic traffic is guaranteed forever.

Search results change. Competitors publish new content. Google's systems change. User behaviour changes.

Google explicitly notes that organic search positions are not static and that there are no guarantees about the outcome of SEO changes. ([Google Search Central – Core Updates](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates))

**SEO is not a "build it once and forget it" channel either.**

---

## The two can work well together

For many businesses, there is no need to choose only one.

The two channels can serve different purposes.

For example:

```text
Google Ads → quick traffic and offer testing
                  ↓
             useful data
                  ↓
SEO → longer-term content and organic visibility
```text

Your Ads campaigns can reveal which searches, offers and messages attract attention.

Those insights can then inform your content strategy.

The reverse can also happen.

If a service page performs well organically, you can use the same customer insight when developing paid campaigns and landing pages.

## Example: a service business in London

Imagine you run a business providing industrial equipment maintenance.

You do not necessarily need to appear for every possible search.

Instead, you could identify a few commercially important services.

### Short term

You could run Google Ads for specific services.

```text
Search → advert → service page → enquiry
```text

You could then measure:

- clicks,
- enquiries,
- search terms,
- cost per enquiry,
- eventual customers.

### Longer term

At the same time, you could build:

- detailed service pages,
- useful guides,
- case studies,
- FAQs.

These can help establish a broader organic presence around the subjects your customers care about.

**Ads can provide faster feedback, while SEO can form part of a longer-term strategy for organic visibility.**

---

## How much should you spend?

There is no universal monthly budget that works for every business.

A small local service company might test Google Ads with a **guide budget of around €1,000 per month**.

Another business might need several thousand euros.

These are **guide prices**, not market rules.

The more useful question is what you can measure.

For example:

```text
€1,000 advertising spend
→ 20 enquiries
→ 5 customers
→ €8,000 revenue
```text

That tells you much more than the advertising spend on its own.

To assess the economics properly, you need to understand the cost of acquiring a customer, the value of that customer and how many leads actually become paying customers.

---

## Be careful with SEO guarantees

SEO is an area where unrealistic promises are particularly common.

If someone tells you:

> "I guarantee you will be number one on Google within three months."

be cautious.

Google explicitly states that no one can guarantee a particular ranking position, and that third-party SEO tools and services cannot guarantee performance either. ([Google Search Central – Third-Party SEO Tools & Advice](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/third-party-seo))

A sensible SEO project should instead explain:

- what will be improved,
- why it needs improving,
- how the work will be measured,
- what content will be created,
- what technical issues were found,
- which business metrics matter.

**You can control the work and the measurement process. You cannot control Google's exact ranking position.**

---

## So which one should you choose?

Here are some useful starting points.

### If you need enquiries quickly

Consider testing Google Ads.

### If you want to build long-term organic visibility

SEO and useful content may be more relevant.

### If you are testing a new service

Ads can provide faster feedback.

### If you want to build a stronger search presence

SEO can become a more important part of the strategy.

### If both matter

Use both, with clearly defined roles.

---

## Start with the business goal, not the Google product

SEO and Google Ads are not competing philosophies.

One is primarily about **building organic search visibility**.

The other is about **buying targeted visibility through advertising**.

SEO tends to be a longer-term activity with no guaranteed rankings.

Google Ads can start producing paid traffic more quickly, but that traffic comes with a direct advertising cost.

So a business does not necessarily need to choose one.

Start by asking:

> Do I need to generate enquiries quickly, or am I building organic visibility for the next few years?

**Once you know the answer, it becomes much easier to decide whether SEO, Google Ads or a combination of both makes sense.**

---

## SEO or Google Ads – which problem should you solve first?

If Google is not currently generating enough enquiries, do not automatically buy an SEO package or launch an Ads campaign. First look at which searches actually matter to your business, whether your website can turn visitors into enquiries, and whether you can measure the journey from click to customer.

A good strategy is not the one that spends the most money on one channel. **It is the one that fits the business objective and gives you measurable information about what happens next.**
