**"How much does a website cost?"**

This is usually the first question a business asks.

But there is another question that matters just as much:

> **"How much could a good website be worth to me?"**

Because a website is not simply a digital business card.

Designed well, it can be:

- a source of new customers,
- a sales channel,
- a quote request system,
- an online shop,
- a booking system,
- a customer service tool,
- an automation platform,
- or even the foundation of how the business runs digitally.

So it is not worth looking at a website's price in isolation.

**The more important question is how much business value it can create.**

---

## How much does a website cost in 2026?

There is no single price for a website.

A simple landing page and a custom shop handling thousands of products can be worlds apart.

Typical ranges in Hungary, in Hungarian forints (HUF):

| Project type                     |           Typical range |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------: |
| Landing page                     |        HUF 70–250 thousand |
| Simple introductory site         |       HUF 150–450 thousand |
| Company website                  |       HUF 300–900 thousand |
| Website with custom design       | HUF 400 thousand–1.5 M+ |
| Simple online shop               |  HUF 500 thousand–1.5 M |
| Complex online shop              |           HUF 1.5–4 M+ |
| Custom web application           |             HUF 1–5 M+ |
| Complex enterprise system        |               HUF 5 M+ |

These are **guide prices**, not fixed rates. The final cost depends on functionality, design, content, integrations, back end, administration and the other needs of the project.

For comparison: according to [1B Telecom's 2026 overview](https://1b.hu/blog/weboldal-keszites-arak-2026-mennyibe-kerul-egy-ceges-weboldal), a simple company website in Hungary typically costs around HUF 250–700 thousand, a company website generally HUF 250–800 thousand, and a more complex project can run into the millions. According to [a 2022 Privátbankár article](https://privatbankar.hu/cikkek/vasarlo/ennyibe-kerul-ha-sajat-webaruhazat-nyitnal.html), building a shop on an open-source system started at around HUF 100–300 thousand, while custom builds rarely came in under half a million.

But the price alone does not tell you whether a website is a good investment.

For that, you need to look at **what it earns back**.

---

## How much is a website worth?

There is no universal figure.

A website's value is determined mainly by **how much business impact it can create**.

Say a business's average customer **spends HUF 100,000**.

If the website brings in just **5 new customers a month**:

```text
5 × HUF 100,000 = HUF 500,000 revenue a month
HUF 500,000 × 12 = HUF 6,000,000 a year
```

This is of course a simple example. The real result depends on the conversion rate, customer value, margin, repeat purchases, and whether those new customers really came because of the website.

But it shows the way of thinking:

> **The question is not whether the website costs HUF 500,000 or HUF 1,000,000. The question is what business result it can create.**

---

## A website does not always make money directly

This is an important distinction.

A website's value does not always show up as:

**visitor → purchase → revenue**

It can have an indirect effect too. For example:

```text
Google search
      ↓
Website
      ↓
References
      ↓
Trust
      ↓
Phone call
      ↓
Business meeting
      ↓
Contract
```

In this case, the website cannot necessarily show the full revenue directly.

Yet it can still be part of the sales process.

---

## How much is a new customer worth?

This is one of the most important numbers a business should know: **customer lifetime value** (CLV). The simple formula, as used by [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/customer-lifetime-value), is average purchase value × purchase frequency × average customer lifespan.

Say a customer's first purchase is **HUF 80,000**, they buy **three times a year** on average, and they stay a customer for **three years** on average.

Their total value is then:

```text
HUF 80,000 × 3 × 3 = HUF 720,000
```

So the value of winning a new customer is not necessarily HUF 80,000.

**It is the value of the whole customer relationship that counts.**

That is why a well-functioning website can be worth far more than the first purchases suggest.

---

## What if the website brings in only one customer a month?

Let us look at it another way. Say:

- the website costs **HUF 600,000**
- the average customer value is **HUF 300,000**
- the website brings in **1 new customer a month**

In this simple model:

```text
HUF 600,000 / HUF 300,000 = 2 customers
```

In other words, once the website brings in two new customers who would not have come otherwise, it has generated revenue equal to its development cost.

And if the website runs for years, the potential return can be much larger.

That is why a website is best treated as an **asset**, not a one-off expense.

---

## But what you build matters

A HUF 300,000 website is not necessarily worse than a HUF 2 million one.

And a HUF 2 million website is not necessarily a better investment.

The question is:

> **What does the website need to achieve?**

For a small local service provider, a simple **5–7 page introductory site** may be more than enough.

A B2B company, on the other hand, may need:

- quote requests,
- CRM integration,
- a customer portal,
- document management,
- automation.

And an online shop needs:

- a product catalogue,
- a basket,
- payments,
- invoicing,
- stock management,
- shipping integrations.

**So a website's value is not set by the amount of code, but by the business problem it solves.**

---

## A good website can create time as well

Not just money.

Say a business receives **10 enquiries a day**.

If every enquiry needs the same basic information sent out, that can mean a lot of admin.

A well-built website can answer in advance:

- What do we offer?
- How much does it cost?
- How does it work?
- Where are we?
- When are we open?
- How can you book an appointment?
- What are the terms of purchase?

If the website partly automates this, it can free up your staff's time.

**Time saved is business value too.**

---

## A website can work around the clock

A salesperson has working hours.\
A shop has opening hours.\
A customer service agent has working hours.

A website, however, is available **on Monday at 10:00, on Saturday at 23:00, on public holidays, or even at night**.

A potential customer can:

- look for information,
- request a quote,
- book an appointment,
- buy a product,
- register,
- send a message.

This can be especially important for businesses whose online processes can be automated.

## A website can create value in marketing too

A website can be the hub of your online marketing. For example:

```text
Google Ads  →  landing page  →  offer  →  lead  →  sale
SEO  →  Google search  →  blog article  →  website  →  enquiry
Instagram / Facebook / TikTok / LinkedIn  →  website  →  contact
```

That way, the website can connect your different marketing channels.

## A website can increase the value of your brand

The image people form of a business comes from many sources.

The website can be an important one.

The design, the copy, the references, the customer reviews and the quality of the information can all shape how a visitor perceives the business.

A professional online presence does not guarantee more sales on its own.

But an outdated, slow or hard-to-use website can stop an interested visitor from taking the next step.

## How much extra revenue can a website bring?

There is no single number for every business.

But it is easy to model. For example:

```text
10,000 visitors / month
        ↓  2% become enquiries
200 leads
        ↓  20% become customers
40 new customers
        ×  HUF 100,000 average customer value
= HUF 4,000,000 revenue a month
```

This is only an **illustrative model**, not a general benchmark. For reference: [Unbounce's 2024 analysis](https://marketingprofs.com/charts/2024/52374/landing-page-conversion-benchmarks) of more than 57 million conversions on over 41,000 landing pages found a median conversion rate of 6.6%, with wide differences between industries, and a campaign page's numbers cannot be projected onto a whole website's traffic.

Every business has to work out its real numbers from its own data.

---

## The important metric is not the number of visitors

You could have 10,000 visitors a day.

If none of them buys, that is of little business value.

You could have 100 visitors a day.

If 10 of them become relevant enquiries, that is much more interesting.

So the more important metrics can be:

**visitors → enquiries → quote requests → customers → revenue**

A website's real value is best judged not by traffic alone, but by **relevant business results**.

---

## ROI – can a website pay for itself?

Yes, but it has to be measured.

The usual formula for return on investment (ROI) is net gain divided by the cost of the investment ([Corporate Finance Institute](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/accounting/return-on-investment-roi-formula/)). Applied to a website:

```text
ROI = (gain attributable to the website – website cost) / website cost × 100
```

Say:

- the website's total cost: **HUF 1,000,000**
- the extra result attributable to the website in a year: **HUF 3,000,000**

The simplified ROI:

```text
(3,000,000 – 1,000,000) / 1,000,000 × 100 = 200%
```

This is only a model; a real calculation also has to account for things like marketing spend, running costs, margin and the uncertainty of attribution.

The point:

> **The question is not whether a website is expensive. It is what business result we expect from it.**

---

## And what does a website cost in the long run?

Development is only one of the costs.

You may also need to budget for:

- a domain,
- hosting,
- email,
- maintenance,
- security updates,
- premium licences,
- SEO,
- content creation,
- advertising,
- further development.

That is why it is worth thinking in terms of **TCO**, total cost of ownership: the estimated cost of purchasing, deploying, managing, using and retiring an asset ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/it-infrastructure/definition/What-is-total-cost-of-ownership-TCO)).

A cheap website can turn out expensive if it:

- keeps breaking,
- is hard to change,
- is not properly optimised,
- needs a lot of manual work,
- or has to be rebuilt from scratch after a few months.

And a more expensive system can be a good investment if it supports the business for years.

---

## When is it not worth spending a lot on a website?

This is just as important a question.

If a business:

- does not win many customers online,
- runs simply,
- does not need complex features,
- lives mainly on recommendations,
- and does not need to sell online,

then it may not need a multi-million custom system.

A simple, well-made website can be far more sensible.

**The goal is not the most expensive website.**

The goal is the right one.

---

## When is it worth investing more?

If the website plays a serious role, directly or indirectly, in winning business, it deserves more attention.

For example, if:

- you sell online,
- you handle many enquiries,
- you advertise,
- you win customers through SEO,
- you operate in several countries,
- you sell complex services,
- you want to automate sales,
- you want a customer portal,
- or the website is one of your most important sales channels.

At that point, the website is no longer a simple cost.

**It is business infrastructure.**

---

## A website's value can grow over time

A website is not a one-off campaign.

If you keep developing it:

```text
content + SEO + references + customer reviews + data + automation + new features
```

your online presence can support more and more of your business processes.

Over time, a well-functioning website can become one of the business's key digital assets.

---

## The question is not what it costs

A website can cost **HUF 200,000**, **HUF 800,000**, **HUF 2,000,000**, or even **HUF 10 million or more**.

Any of them can be justified – and any of them can be a bad investment.

The deciding question is:

> **What does the website need to create for the business?**

More enquiries?\
More sales?\
Less admin?\
Faster customer handling?\
A stronger brand?\
Automated processes?\
A new business model?

Once that is clear, the right technology and budget can be defined.

---

## A good website is not an expense. It is a business tool.

Not every business needs custom development.

Not every business needs an online shop.

And not every business needs a multi-million website.

But if a website can regularly win new customers, support sales, save time or automate a business process, its value should not be compared only with its development price.

**The question is not whether a website costs HUF 500,000 or HUF 1,500,000.**

**The question is what you get for it – and what value it can create for your business.**
