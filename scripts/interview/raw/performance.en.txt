### Q1
LEVEL: junior
TOPIC: Latency, throughput & percentiles
TYPE: theory
QUESTION: During a load test of an API, the average response time is 180 ms, the p95 is 420 ms, and the p99 is 1.8 s. What does this result most strongly indicate?
A) Most requests complete in exactly 180 ms or less.
B) Response times have significant variability, and a smaller portion of requests are very slow.
C) The system is definitely CPU-bound.
D) The p99 value is invalid because it cannot be higher than the average.
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The average is only an aggregate metric, while p95 indicates that approximately 95% of requests completed within about 420 ms. A p99 of 1.8 seconds indicates significant delays among the slowest few percent of requests. The result alone does not prove CPU saturation; additional metrics are needed for that. The common wrong answer is tempting because it is easy to interpret the average as the system's general response time.
###END

### Q2
LEVEL: junior
TOPIC: Load, stress, soak & spike testing
TYPE: scenario
QUESTION: A webshop normally receives 100 requests/sec. The team wants to verify how the system behaves during a sudden, short-lived campaign launch, when traffic jumps from 100 to 1000 requests/sec and then drops back after a few minutes. Which type of test best describes this goal?
A) Soak test
B) Load test
C) Spike test
D) Stress test
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: A spike test examines the system's response to a sudden, significant change in load, such as a sharp increase in traffic, including whether the system can handle the surge and recover afterward. A soak test focuses on sustained load over a long period. A load test typically evaluates expected normal load, while a stress test examines behavior near or beyond the system's capacity limits. A load test is tempting because the example does involve HTTP load, but the key characteristic here is the sudden change.
###END

B) Throughput will always decrease immediately.
C) p95 cannot be used in performance testing.

### Q13

TOPIC: JVM & GC tuning
TYPE: behavior
QUESTION: A Java application's heap is 2 GB. During a performance test, GC runs frequently, and heap usage repeatedly approaches the maximum. Which conclusion is most appropriate?
